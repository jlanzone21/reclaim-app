Browser tracking

Install the Reclaim browser extension to let the web version notice risky browsing patterns and check in with you — the same idea as the Android app, for your computer. Everything stays inside this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the extension makes no network requests at all.

For now it's installed by hand: in Chrome or Edge open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, choose Load unpacked, and pick the extension folder from the Reclaim project. Then reload this page.

When this is on, the extension notes which site you're on and for how long (never the page address beyond the site name), and checks page text for a short list of words — keeping only which word matched, never the text. It never reads webmail, banking, health or messaging pages. Notifications are always generic; the detail only shows here, once Reclaim is open.

Before you begin

Reclaim helps you find scripture, community, accountability, and counseling for struggles with pornography. It's a starting point, not a solution on its own.

  • This app does not provide therapy, diagnosis, or clinical advice.
  • It is never a substitute for a real pastor, counselor, or accountability partner.
  • Sample resources in this build use placeholder (555) numbers — real local resources should be added before relying on this app.
  • Reclaim's AI runs entirely on your device, so your conversations never leave it. Turning it on downloads it once (about 1 GB).
  • Check-in data you log stays on this device (local storage) and is not sent anywhere.

If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988 right now, or call 911.

How Reclaim works

Everything here runs on this device — see Privacy for the full detail on what's stored and why.

The five tabs
  • Chat — talk through what's going on; the AI can point you to a Bible verse, a devotional, a small group, a counselor, or your accountability partner.
  • Check-In — log a moment, resisted or slipped, with what was going on.
  • Home — a verse, one-tap call shortcuts for your accountability partner(s), and this page.
  • Insights — your own check-in trends and (once granted) on-device activity, over time.
  • Privacy — turn any permission on or off, manage which apps get read for patterns, and edit your setup answers any time.
Turning on permissions

Every permission is optional and off by default — Reclaim works with none of them granted, just without the pattern-noticing that powers risk check-in notifications. Grant only what you're comfortable with, any time, from Privacy.

For most of them, tapping Grant shows the normal "Allow?" prompt right there. Three of them — App usage, Notification access, and Accessibility — are more sensitive, so Android requires turning them on from a system settings list instead of an in-app switch. Tapping Grant for those takes you straight to the right list; from there, find Reclaim in it and turn it on yourself, then come back to the app.

Once permissions are on
  • Roughly every 15 minutes, while your phone is unlocked and in use, Reclaim records one small local snapshot from whichever permissions you've granted.
  • Only apps you've explicitly added to your allowlist (Privacy) ever have their on-screen text read and checked for flagged words — every other app is noticed by name only.
  • If enough signals line up, Reclaim sends a check-in notification — a lighter suggestion (like reading a verse) for a lower score, or a direct "Call [name]" action to an accountability partner for a high one.
  • Being on Reclaim itself, or having opened it recently, actually lowers that score rather than counting against you.
